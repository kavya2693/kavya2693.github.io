<S n="01" t="Problem">

A large retail group replenishes **4,000+ SKUs across 33 stores** every day. Too little stock loses the sale; too much ties up cash and spoils. One model per SKU-store series means more than 130,000 models to train and monitor, and naive forecasts ("same day last week") ignore promotions, seasonality and the way one product steals demand from another.

</S>

<S n="02" t="Context">

- **Users:** replenishment, production-planning and inventory-allocation teams, and the downstream planning applications that call the forecasts.
- **Requirement:** a forecast per SKU × store, with a confidence indicator and model metadata, available through an API.
- **Not GenAI.** This is classical applied ML, where a number has to be right every morning.

</S>

<S n="03" t="Data">

Daily sales history per SKU × store, promotions, calendar effects, product and store attributes. For the research rebuild, a product/store graph adds substitutes, complements, store similarity and proximity.

The rebuild runs on **simulated data** at demo scale — 6 stores × 200 SKUs × 730 days — with a time-based holdout and a 7-day embargo between train and test:

| Split | Rows |
|---|---|
| Train | 777,600 |
| Validation | 8,400 |
| Test | 55,200 |

</S>

<Illustrative>Illustrative · feature construction pattern, synthetic column names</Illustrative>

```python
def add_features(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = df.sort_values(["store", "sku", "date"])
    g = df.groupby(["store", "sku"])["units"]
    for lag in (1, 7, 14, 28):
        df[f"lag_{lag}"] = g.shift(lag)                 # only past values: no leakage
    for w in (7, 28):
        past = g.shift(1)
        df[f"rmean_{w}"] = past.groupby([df.store, df.sku]).transform(lambda s: s.rolling(w).mean())
        df[f"rstd_{w}"] = past.groupby([df.store, df.sku]).transform(lambda s: s.rolling(w).std())
    df["item_age"] = (df["date"] - df.groupby("sku")["date"].transform("min")).dt.days
    return df
```

</S>

<S n="06" t="Modeling / AI approach">

- **One global model** instead of thousands of local ones: series share statistical strength, and new SKUs borrow from similar ones.
- **A p90 quantile head** alongside the point forecast, because safety stock is a quantile question, not a mean question.
- **A three-arm ablation** for the graph hypothesis: seasonal-naive, LightGBM without graph features, LightGBM with them. Cold-start subsets by item age isolate where graph context should help most.

</S>

<S n="07" t="Evaluation">

Research rebuild, simulated data, test set:

| Arm | Test WMAPE |
|---|---|
| Seasonal-naive | 0.2273 |
| LightGBM (no graph) | 0.1838 |
| LightGBM + graph features | +0.4% better than without |

- On ultra-cold items (under 14 days old, n = 1,662) the graph lift rises to **+2.1%**.
- Graph features account for **0.3%** of total LightGBM gain; rolling statistics and item age dominate.
- The p90 head covers **88.5%** of outcomes against a 90% target — slightly under-covering, which matters for stock-outs.

</S>

<S n="08" t="Production considerations">

- Forecasts are served with model metadata so a planner can see which model version produced a number.
- Promotion is gated: a retrained model that does not beat the validation bar is not promoted.
- Under-coverage of the p90 head is the number to watch in production — it translates directly into stock-outs.

</S>

<S n="09" t="Results">

- Production forecasting across 4,000+ SKUs × 33 stores, served by API and refreshed automatically.
- In the rebuild: **~19% lower WMAPE than seasonal-naive** (0.2273 → 0.1838).
- Graph features: a small, honest effect — +0.4% overall, +2.1% for brand-new items.
- Earlier, at Noon: daily delivery-demand MAPE cut from **25% to 18%** across e-commerce, grocery and quick-commerce.

</S>

<S n="10" t="Tradeoffs / lessons">

- **The boring features win.** Rolling means, volatility and item age carry most of the signal.
- **Report the small lift.** +0.4% is a real result; dressing it up would make every other number on this page less credible.
- **Graph context earns its place at the edges** — new products with no history — not in the average.

<Note>The model is the easy part. The feature pipeline, the gate and the coverage of the p90 are where forecasts succeed or fail.</Note>

</S>
