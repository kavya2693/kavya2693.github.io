<S n="01" t="Problem">

In pharma, AI may only recommend or generate content that has passed medical, legal and regulatory (MLR) review — and someone must be able to prove which approved claim each sentence came from. Market-access teams also present the same reimbursement evidence in a different format for every country: adapting one dossier took **about three months**, so smaller markets waited quarters before patients could get the drug reimbursed.

</S>

<S n="02" t="Context">

- **Users:** brand, field and market-access teams; MLR reviewers; regional affiliates.
- **Constraint:** generative AI is fast but ungoverned; regulated content is governed but slow. Without lineage, nothing generated is usable.
- **Goal:** recommendations and generated documents built only from approved content, with lineage that survives an audit.

</S>

<S n="03" t="Data">

- **Customer signals:** profile, specialty and engagement history.
- **Content metadata:** asset, key message, claim, supporting evidence, approval status and approval ID, market, expiry.
- **Lineage edges:** which slide or message used which claim, which claim rests on which evidence, which approval covers it.

</S>

<S n="06" t="Modeling / AI approach">

- **Constrain, don't filter.** The generator is populated only from approved content, instead of generating freely and checking afterwards.
- **Lineage is a graph.** Impact ("what changes if this claim is withdrawn?") is a descendants query; provenance ("where did this sentence come from?") is an ancestors query:

<Code lang="python" title="commercial-metadata-catalog/src/catalog.py" href="https://github.com/kavya2693/commercial-metadata-catalog/blob/main/src/catalog.py#L31-L48" code={`def build_lineage(edges):
    g = nx.DiGraph()
    for e in edges:
        g.add_edge(e["from"], e["to"], via=e["via"])
    return g


def impact(g, node):
    return sorted(nx.descendants(g, node)) if node in g else []


def provenance(g, node):
    return sorted(nx.ancestors(g, node)) if node in g else []


def sources_reached(g, node):
    """Upstream nodes that are themselves sources (no upstream of their own)."""
    return [n for n in nx.ancestors(g, node) if g.in_degree(n) == 0] if node in g else []`} />

<S n="07" t="Evaluation">

- The governance checks run like tests: every dataset owned and classified, every KPI traced to a source, every generated slide traced through key message → MLR approval → content → source asset.
- In the public generator demo, three approved claims render and the one claim still in review is refused — a mechanism check, not a production benchmark.
- Withdrawing an approved claim lists exactly which assets it touches.

</S>

<S n="08" t="Production considerations">

- Served through an API (FastAPI in the public rebuild) so every downstream tool inherits the same gate.
- Lineage is written into the artifact itself (speaker notes) and a sidecar record, so it survives when a file is emailed out of the system.

</S>

<S n="09" t="Results">

- Per-market dossier adaptation: **~3 months → ~1 week.**
- Rolled across **~25 markets**, including **6 low- and middle-income countries** that had previously waited longest.
- Slide-level lineage from generated output to source evidence and approval.

</S>

<S n="10" t="Tradeoffs / lessons">

- **Lineage at the slide level, not the document level.** Document-level lineage cannot answer the question an auditor actually asks.
- **Generation is only as fast as review.** Carrying the approval ID with every piece of content is what lets a reviewer check a draft instead of re-researching it.

<Note>In regulated GenAI, lineage is the product. Speed only becomes usable once every sentence can be traced.</Note>

</S>
